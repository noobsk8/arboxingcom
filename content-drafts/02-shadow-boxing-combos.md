## Introduction

Shadow boxing gives you a way to practice punches, defense, rhythm, and movement without a bag or partner. The goal is not to throw as hard as possible. The goal is to stay balanced, see the imaginary target, return to guard, and move with intent.

Try these combinations in an open space where you can move safely. Use an orthodox stance for the left/right examples below, or adapt the lead and rear sides to your stance.

## Three combinations to start with

### 1-2: Find your rhythm

Throw the lead jab and rear cross, then reset your feet and guard.

**AR Boxing sequence:** `ljh-rch`

### 1-1-2: Build volume

Use two jabs to establish distance before the cross. Keep both jabs straight and bring the hand home each time.

**AR Boxing sequence:** `ljh-ljh-rch`

### 1-2-3-slip-2: Punch, defend, return

Finish the 1-2-3, make a small slip, and return with the cross. The defensive movement is part of the combination, not an afterthought.

**AR Boxing sequence:** `ljh-rch-lhh-sl-rch`

## A beginner three-round format

Round one is technique: move slowly and focus on your guard. Round two is rhythm: repeat each sequence without stopping. Round three is movement: add a small step or angle after the final punch while staying balanced.
