## Why add defense to a combination?

A boxing combination should not always end with the last punch. Adding a slip, roll, or guard movement gives you a reset and helps you practice what happens after you attack.

Keep defensive movements small and controlled. A slip moves your head off the center line; a roll bends the knees and moves under an imagined hook. Neither movement should make you lose your stance.

## Three combinations to practice

### 1-2-slip-2

Throw the jab-cross, slip left, and counter with the rear cross.

**AR Boxing sequence:** `ljh-rch-sl-rch`

### 1-2-roll-3

Throw the jab-cross, roll under an imagined return hook, and come back with the lead hook.

**AR Boxing sequence:** `ljh-rch-rolll-lhh`

### 1-2-3-slip-2

Build from the classic 1-2-3, then move your head and finish with the rear cross.

**AR Boxing sequence:** `ljh-rch-lhh-sl-rch`

## How to train the sequence

Practice each combination in three stages: punches only, punches plus defense at half speed, then the complete sequence at a steady pace. Reset your guard before starting again. If your balance or posture breaks down, slow the movement down.

England Boxing coaching guidance emphasizes finishing combinations balanced and ready for movement or a change of angle. The examples here are practice prompts, not a replacement for coaching.
