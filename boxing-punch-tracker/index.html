What it does and does not measure

Camera-guided training, not punch-power hardware.

AR Boxing detects interaction with supported workout targets and movement prompts. It does not measure punch velocity, punch power, bag impact force, or total punch output like dedicated wearable or bag-mounted trackers.

It also does not certify boxing technique, diagnose form issues, or replace qualified boxing instruction.

Lighting, phone placement, distance from the camera, clothing, and movement speed can affect camera-based tracking.

FAQ

Camera boxing app questions.

Is there a boxing app that uses the iPhone camera?

Yes. AR Boxing uses the iPhone front camera for supported workout target interactions. Set up your phone, choose a session, and respond to punch and defense prompts as you train.

What movements can I practice?

Supported prompts include jabs, crosses, hooks, uppercuts, body punches, slips, rolls, and guard movements. Recognition is used for interactive targets; it does not certify that you performed the technique correctly.

Do I need an Apple Watch, wrist tracker, or bag sensor?

No. Your iPhone and enough clear training space are the core requirements. You can use the interactive shadowboxing workouts without a punching bag.

Where should I put my phone?

Use a stable position where the front camera can see your upper body and movements. Allow space to punch and move without contacting the phone, furniture, or other people. Lighting and distance can affect recognition.

Does it measure punch power or analyze my technique?

No. AR Boxing does not measure punch power, velocity, or total punch output, and it does not certify boxing form. It provides interactive targets and workout feedback; qualified coaching remains useful for technique.
